# Sablière — a KZ2 circuit designed backwards from where the pass happens

1,302.56 m · 7 named corners (9 arc segments; T1 and T2 are compound) · 14.59 m of
elevation · 42.92 s forward, 42.61 s reversed · Grade 1

---

## The argument

I was told to start from the overtaking spots and let the rest of the lap be whatever
gets you between them. So the first thing I did was throw away the assumption that
usually organises a track design — that an overtaking spot is a long straight into a
heavy braking zone. On this kart it cannot be, and the numbers say so flatly. At the
measured 1.53 g the longest braking zone that can exist anywhere on this circuit is
48.5 m, and that is the extreme case — a downhill run at 143.7 km/h into a 47 km/h
hairpin, and it only exists in the reverse direction. The two designated passing
corners brake for 18.8 m and 20.8 m. Twenty metres. You cannot out-brake anybody from
100 m back because there is no 100 m.

So an overtaking spot here is made of three things, and the third one is the only one
that is really under a designer's control:

1. **A tow.** 198 m and 195 m, which is as long as the regulation allows (200 m max on
   any straight) and is therefore not a design decision at all — it is the ceiling.
2. **An entry wide enough for two lines.** 12 m at both passing corners, widened
   *outward* over 50 m so the inside edge stays put. 8 m is the Grade 1 floor;
   REFERENCES.md itself calls widening the overtaking corners a free plausibility gain,
   and it is the one lever that costs nothing.
3. **An exit that makes the pass hurt for a long time.** This is where the design lives.

And then the thing that made the whole layout fall out: **the two passing corners are
the same two corners in both directions**, because each of them has a long straight on
*both* sides. T1 has the 198 m start straight before it and the 195 m back straight
after it. T2 has the back straight before it and 276.7 m of uninterrupted full throttle
after it. A corner flanked by long straights is a passing corner whichever way you
drive it — reversing the lap only swaps which side is the tow and which is the
punishment. That is the constraint that usually kills a racecraft-led design (find two
*more* passing spots for the reverse layout), and this geometry does not have to solve
it. It inherits it.

The two corners are then made **opposite in sense**, so the reverse lap is a different
circuit rather than the same one backwards:

| | forward | reversed |
|---|---|---|
| **T1 The Anvil** | R32 → R50, *opening* | R50 → R32, *closing* |
| **T2 The Kettle** | R50 → R32, *closing* | R32 → R50, *opening* |

Each direction gets exactly one clean pass-and-drive-away corner and exactly one
dive-bomb. They just trade places.

## The number this circuit is built on

Here is the measurement that made the design specific to *this* kart rather than
generic. At T1, three lines:

| line | radius | speed | what it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| free racing line | 48.85 m | **105.4 km/h** | 12 m of road, outside–apex–outside, 1.79 g |
| defensive compromise | 42.4 m | **95.8 km/h** | gave up the outer third, still alone |
| pinned to the inside | 27.0 m | **66.7 km/h** | a kart genuinely alongside, wheel can't open |

A 38.7 km/h penalty for being pinned. In a car that number is ten or twelve. Here it is
nearly forty, because holding a 27 m radius costs steering lock and lock is the one
thing this kart cannot afford (#137). Then play it out over the 195 m back straight:

```
  outside line   (R 40 m)  exit  92.2 km/h  →  137.5 km/h at T2's braking point, 5.779 s
  pinned inside  (R 27 m)  exit  66.7 km/h  →  135.2 km/h at T2's braking point, 6.331 s
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  the outside kart is 0.553 s and 21.1 m ahead by the time they brake again
```

**So on this circuit the switchback is the dominant move and the outside line wins
side-by-side corners.** That is not how racing normally reads, it is a direct
consequence of an open defect, and it is the single most interesting thing about
driving here. A defender who covers the inside at T1 wins the corner and loses the
straight. The counter-move is at T2, 195 m later, which is why the two corners are
where they are.

It also means **contact is survivable rather than a lottery**, which the brief asked
for: with a locked axle a kart shoved off line mid-corner loses everything, so the
answer is width. 12 m through both passing corners is what gives the yielded kart a
40 m radius instead of running out of asphalt at 36.

## The lap, corner by corner, as a driver meets it

**Start/finish straight — 198 m, +0.5%, 9 m widening to 12.**
You cross the line at about 100 km/h in 4th, still gathering from T7, and the road is
dead flat. It is meant to be: a gradient change at 138 km/h needs a 1,270 m vertical
radius, and the whole lap is 1,302 m. The last 50 m widen outward and you can see the
extra asphalt arrive on your right. Braking board at 50 m. Peak 135.8 km/h.

**T1 — The Anvil. 98° right, R32 into R50, 12 m.**
Eighteen metres of brake. That is all you get. Turn in early, take the tighter first
half at 105 km/h, and let the R50 second half unwind you onto the back straight. If
someone is inside you, do not fight it — take the outside, accept being second at the
apex, and take the 21 m back before T2. If you *are* the one inside and someone stays
alongside, you are on a 27 m radius at 66.7 km/h and you are going to watch them go.

**Back straight — 195 m, −0.5%.** Twelve metres tapering to nine tapering back to
twelve. 137.8 km/h, top gear, and this is the fastest the kart ever goes in this
direction — it never reaches its 143.9 km/h top speed anywhere on the forward lap,
because no straight is long enough. That is the 200 m regulation doing exactly the job
it exists to do.

**T2 — The Kettle. 100° right, R50 into R32, 12 m.**
The open half invites you to throw the kart at it. Do, but know that the second half
closes to R32 and that is where the road runs out for whoever is on the outside. Exit
at 104.5 km/h onto the longest drive of the lap. **This is where a pass sticks** — 276.7 m
of full throttle before the next braking point, 59% longer than what a T1 pass buys
you, and no corner in between where the road gives it back.

**Rim approach — 139.5 m.** Thirty metres in, the road drops away. This is the lap's
only crest and it is laid at a 950 m radius — 4.6% over the legal minimum, held tight
on purpose — so at 117 km/h it takes about a tenth of a g off the kart. It is the
biggest single load event elevation produces anywhere on the circuit, and by the time
you have felt it you are already committed to what comes next.

**T3 — The Rim. 95° left, R55, 9 m, falling at 4%.**
The corner. 130.3 km/h on a 71.8 m racing line is **1.86 g — the measured sustained
ceiling — held for 2.52 seconds**, in a left-hander, which is the 2.43 g rollover side.
A 23% margin, the smallest anywhere on the property in either direction, and it is
downhill and you cannot see the exit from the entry. This is where #32's inside-rear
lift should be visible, and it is the corner that decides whether any future change to
the tire model ships. Do not add lock. Adding lock here is how you find out what the
run-off is like.

**The Plunge — 88.5 m, −4%.** 136.6 km/h and 42.5 m of braking, the longest in this
direction, and in the last 13.5 m of it the road compresses into the valley floor —
deliberately inside the braking zone, at 1.69× the minimum radius, for about a tenth of
a g of extra load exactly when you want it.

**T4 — The Cellar. 175° right, R16, 10 m, 13.7 m below the start line.**
47.5 km/h, second gear, and the only place on the lap that is on the wrong side of #137
on purpose. Unlike the test track's 11 m hairpin you can get round it — a 19.6 m line at
0.91 g — but you feel the kart scrubbing and you feel it get worse if you arrive hot.
Then it is a launch: 111.75 m of 4.38% climb, up through the box, out of a locked axle.

**T5 — The Ramp. 30° right, R90.** Flat. It is a placement problem, not a grip problem.

**Upper climb → T6 — The Shelf. 50° left, R30, 9 m.**
119.1 km/h at 1.86 g through a nine-metre road, over a crest, exit blind. **Do not fight
here.** Pinned on the inside is 65.4 km/h — a 53.7 km/h loss — and you then have to
reach T7 and the start straight from there. The narrowness is the design.

**Gate approach → T7 — The Gate. 102° right, R28, 10 m.**
94.2 km/h, the slowest thing on the lap outside the hairpin, and it exists to be slow.
A slow last corner concertinas the field onto the longest straight, and a tow into T1
is the whole reason T1 works. Defending here costs 35 km/h and you pay it out over 198 m.

## Reversed, which is a genuinely different circuit

Three things change, and they are not cosmetic.

**T4 becomes the best corner on the property.** Reversed, the Cellar climb is a 111.75 m
descent at −4.38% on which the kart reaches its full **143.7 km/h** — the only place in
either direction that it does — with a **48.5 m braking zone**, the longest anywhere, into
the same 10 m hairpin. And the exit is onto 319 m of climbing full throttle. Longest
tow-in and longest drive-out on the same corner. That is a textbook overtaking spot and
it does not exist in the forward lap. It is the strongest single argument for shipping
the reverse layout: it is not a variation, it contains something the original does not.

**T3 stops being T3.** Forward it is a flat-out downhill left held at the grip ceiling.
Reversed it is a *climbing, opening right* taken from 110 to 128 km/h under power. Same
asphalt, different corner in every sense a driver cares about.

**Every hand inverts, so the rollover side inverts.** The forward lap keeps its big
sustained loads on the 2.81 g right-hand side except at T3 and T6; the reverse lap puts
them on the 2.43 g left. The reverse layout is measurably closer to tipping the kart and
has to be validated separately rather than inferred from the forward run.

Reverse's passing spots: **T4**, then **T2** (now opening, 139.5 m climbing tow, exits onto
the back straight), then **T1** (now closing, 195 m tow, exits onto the start straight).
Three, against forward's two-and-a-half.

### What does not survive the reversal

Seven things, and the list is the point — ADR-0046 says reverse is an authored layout
and this is the evidence for why:

1. **The pit lane does not reverse at all.** A deceleration lane peeling off at 25° is a
   155° merge in the other direction, and the cap is 30°. The reverse layout needs its
   own asphalt stubs, not just its own furniture placement.
2. **T4's run-off is sized for the wrong speed.** Total brake failure arrives at 136.6 km/h
   forward and 143.7 reversed; the honest gravel requirements are 91.7 m and 101.5 m and
   neither is buildable. 14 m is built and the rest is handed to a Type A air fence. This
   is the weakest-sourced thing in the whole document and I would rather say so than
   quietly pick a number.
3. **Exit kerbs become entry kerbs.** Apex kerbs sit on a corner's inside and survive
   untouched. Everything else is authored twice.
4. **Sector marks do not translate.** Equal-*time* thirds fall at 470.5 / 850.5 m forward
   and at 825.1 / 461.6 m (forward coordinates) reversed. Both land on straights in both
   directions — checked, not assumed; one of them clears T2b's exit by 24.1 m.
5. **The grid mirrors.** Pole is right-column forward (T1 is a right) and left-column
   reversed (T7 is a left).
6. **Braking boards are all on the wrong side.**
7. **Gearing.** 137.8 km/h forward, 143.7 reversed. One final drive cannot be right for both.

## What the elevation is actually for

14.587 m of range, closing to 5×10⁻⁵ m, all of it in the slow half of the lap because
that is the only place the regulation permits it. The plateau — start straight, T1, back
straight, T2 — is flat to ±0.5%. Then one crest, a constant 4% descent through the
fastest corner on the circuit into the quarry floor, and a 4.4–5% climb back out.

And a finding that came out of doing the arithmetic and which I think matters more than
the layout does. **R = V²/K is algebraically a cap on vertical acceleration.** Since
a_vert = v²/R and R_min = V²/K, at the minimum legal radius the speed cancels:

```
    a_vert = K / 12.96  m/s²     (V in km/h)

    convex,  K = 15  →  1.157 m/s²  =  0.118 g   over any crest, at any speed
    concave, K = 20  →  1.543 m/s²  =  0.157 g   in any compression, at any speed
```

So the maximum load swing a legal Grade 1 kart circuit can produce, anywhere, is
−11.8% / +15.7% of static — and the same article then tells you to *increase* R in
approach, release, braking and curved sections, which reduces it further. ARCHITECTURE.md
§11 says elevation is there "both for looks and because it loads the tires." The second
half is nearly wrong, and the regulation the project just finished sourcing is what
proves it. Elevation on a kart circuit is a **visibility** feature. Use it to hide corner
exits, not to load tires.

Eight vertical curves on this lap. All eight sit inside straights. None is inside a
corner. Two are inside braking zones and both are stated as choices, with the radius
margin, rather than left for a reviewer to notice.

## What I would tell someone about to drive it for the first time

**Do not defend.** That is the whole briefing and it is genuinely counter-intuitive. If
someone gets alongside you at T1 or T2, let them have the inside and take the outside
line. You will be second at the apex and twenty-one metres ahead by the next braking
point. Fighting for the inside on this kart costs you thirty-eight km/h and there is a
195 m straight immediately afterwards for you to think about it on.

**The two corners after the line are one problem, not two.** T1 is where you take the
place. T2 is where you find out if you keep it. If you make a move at T1 and it feels
clean, you have about five and a half seconds before it gets tested.

**T3 is not a corner you attack.** It is 1.86 g for two and a half seconds in a
left-hander, downhill, and you cannot see the exit. Whatever the kart is doing when you
turn in is what it will be doing when you come out. Adding steering lock in there does
not tighten the line — it starts the scrub, and you have 91 metres of arc to do it in.

**The bottom of the Plunge is the one place elevation does something.** The road
compresses under the brakes. It is worth about a tenth of a g and it is the only place
you will feel the ground push back.

**T6 is the corner people crash at.** It is fast, it is narrow, and the exit is over a
crest. It is not a passing place and anyone who treats it as one loses T7, the start
straight and the whole lap.

**And drive it backwards.** It is not the same track. The hairpin at the bottom of the
quarry becomes the best braking zone in the game — you will hit 143.7 km/h getting to
it, which you never do in the other direction — and the fast left that terrifies you at
T3 becomes a climbing right you get on the power in.

## Where this design is weak, said plainly

It rests on **#137**, which is an open defect. If the lock cliff is fixed and the kart
holds 1.86 g at any radius, the outside-versus-pinned comparison at T1 goes from
92.2 / 66.7 km/h to 96.9 / 79.5, and the switchback stops being automatic. The circuit
still works — the widths, tows and exit drives are all still right — but the thing that
makes it *interesting* is contingent, and every number here should be re-measured the
day that ticket closes.

It also rests on a **slipstream that does not exist.** `src/core/vehicle.h` has one drag
term, per kart, with no awareness of any other kart, and nothing in the docs or the
source mentions a tow. At 138 km/h the drag on this kart is 617 N; a 25% reduction in
the tow is worth up to 4.5 m/s of closing over 195 m, which is exactly the difference
between arriving alongside and arriving behind. Without it every layout in this exercise
loses its passing spots and the field runs in a procession. That is not a track problem
and it is not mine to fix, but it is the largest single threat to the proposal and it is
in the JSON's `outside_scope_concerns` with the arithmetic.

And **T1 and T2 are the same hand**, separated by one straight, which means the kart that
yields the inside at T1 exits on the left of the back straight — already the correct side
for T2's entry. The switchback may be rewarded twice and a T1 pass may turn out to be
worth nothing. The mitigation is that T2's entry is the open R50 half, so a kart on the
inside can still brake later and make the first apex. That is an argument, not a
measurement, and it takes two AI karts to settle.
